# Law and Economics (BS)

---

The Bachelor of Science in Law and Economics is a 56-unit major available to current USC students who are interested in gaining a deep, interdisciplinary understanding of how the law structures and influences economic activity, and how economic reasoning and empirical analysis can illuminate the operation of law. By combining rigorous training in legal reasoning, economic theory and quantitative methods, this major equips students to evaluate how law shapes markets, incentives and policy outcomes, and how economic forces, in turn, inform the creation and interpretation of legal rules.

Students in this distinctive program gain comprehensive knowledge of the economic and regulatory complexities of the digital era, understanding the economic consequences of innovation such as AI, its effect on labor markets and productivity, as well as the legal challenges it poses in areas such as privacy, data security, intellectual property and antitrust laws law.

As future leaders in both the public and private sectors, students in this program will learn to think critically and analytically about the frameworks that govern society’s allocation of rights and economic resources. The major fosters an integrated approach to understanding how legal institutions, economic models and human behavior interact, allowing students to apply both legal and economic reasoning to complex regulatory, social, political and business challenges.

Current USC students must have a cumulative GPA of 3.0 and must submit a personal statement to the Gould School of Law’s Office of Undergraduate Programs for review.

## General Education Requirements

---

Eight courses in six Core Literacies, plus two courses in Global Perspectives (which may double-count with courses in the Core Literacies). In addition, all entering freshmen are expected to complete a General Education Seminar during their first year at USC. These seminars satisfy one of the Core Literacy requirements.

Core Literacies:

GE-A: The Arts (one course)

GE-B: Humanistic Inquiry (two courses)

GE-C: Social Analysis (two courses)

GE-D: Life Sciences (one course)

GE-E: Physical Sciences (one course)

GE-F: Quantitative Reasoning (one course)

Global Perspectives:

GE-G: Global Perspectives I (one course)

GE-H: Global Perspectives II (one course)
